Chapter I — Born Where Spirits Listen

Ta-Za was born where the mountains held their breath. His first cry rose into cold starlight, and the elders felt the old ones stir. His hair was white as snow untouched by foot or blood.

Animals lingered near him. Fires burned steady in his presence. The elders spoke softly, for children like this belonged not to time, but to balance.

"He walks with one foot in memory."
I
Chapter II — The Night of Blood and Paw

Rifles shattered prayer. Fire devoured cedar and hide. Ta-Za ran toward death without thought.

When his blood touched the snow, the guardian answered. Bone bent. Fur rose. The White Cat stood where mercy had been broken.

"He did not hunt. He corrected."
II
Chapter III — The Gentle Warrior

Control was learned through pain. Prayer through loss. Each transformation cost him something he could never reclaim.

Men spoke of a silver-haired warrior who calmed storms with his voice, and a white shadow that followed cruelty home.

"Strength that does not roar endures longest."
III
Chapter IV — Exile of the White Cat

The elders sent him away so the world would not devour him. Rails cut the earth. Smoke replaced prayer.

Ta-Za became a story that followed lonely roads — a reminder that the land still remembered who it belonged to.

"A guardian without a home guards everything."
IV
Chapter V — The White Guardian, Now

Now he walks between eras. Soft-spoken. Watchful. Beautiful in a way that warns rather than invites.

When injustice bleeds into soil, the White Guardian answers — not in rage, but in balance.

"As long as the land remembers, so will he."
V
The Spirit Testament

If you read this page, you walk close to the veil. Know this — guardians are not born to be loved, but to endure.

Should you hear padded steps where no beast walks, do not run. Offer respect. The White Guardian watches still.

"Balance is never loud."
